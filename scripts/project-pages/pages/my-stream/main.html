My Stream

A web app that pulls articles from RSS feeds, has an AI model score each one, and drafts only the ones that clear a quality threshold, in English and Arabic. Publishing is left to an editor.

Model calls cost money, and cheaper models score lower on the same rubric. So anything that can be rejected without a model call is rejected first, each remaining item gets one scoring call, and the threshold moves with the model, so one platform setting holds across seven providers.

1Start the run in the background

A run starts from the article queue's Run Full Cycle button as one GraphQL mutation. The Lambda records a job, hands the work to a second invocation of itself, and returns the job id straight away.

  • A request id seen in the last 90 seconds returns the existing job, so a double click or a retried request doesn't start a second run.
  • Before touching an article, the worker loads the workspace's AI profile and sends the chosen model a one-token probe. A retired model, an exhausted quota or a rejected key fails the job with a specific message; a transient error lets it continue.
  • The page polls the job record every 1.5 seconds and stops waiting after 15 minutes, the Lambda's own time limit. A cancel request is checked before scoring and again before drafting.

The other way in is the daily schedule. At 08:00 UTC, workspaces set to daily or weekdays get 1 to 10 articles written from their own topic list. That path skips scraping, scoring and the threshold. Its topic is still checked against the blocklist.

2Scrape, then drop what's already known

Every workspace gets the platform's shared RSS feeds, 13 in the code's defaults, plus any it adds. Before a model sees an item, two checks that cost nothing remove what shouldn't be scored.

  • Already drafted: the item's URL is normalised (lower-case host, tracking parameters and trailing slash removed) and compared with the source URLs of existing articles.
  • Blocked: admin patterns match the URL, the title or the body, as exact, prefix or contains.
  • Near-duplicates within the run are dropped by link, by title, or by more than 85% word overlap between titles.
  • Items with 1,000 characters of text or fewer get the full page fetched, three at a time.

3Score every item on one fixed rubric

Up to a per-run cap, each queued item gets one model call with a fixed system prompt that asks for relevance, quality and trending, each out of 100. The workspace's voice and keywords don't enter scoring.

  • The overall score is 0.5 × relevance + 0.4 × quality + 0.1 × trending.
  • Before that, the code takes 10 quality points per warning sign, up to 30: clickbait phrasing, a title mostly in capitals, more than one exclamation mark, under 50 characters of text, or a speculative "will …?" title.
  • The cap is 5 items by default, and results are cached for 24 hours per Lambda instance.

4Hold anything below the line

The platform sets one quality threshold. The workspace's chosen model shifts it by a fixed offset, because cheaper models score lower on the same rubric, and the result is clamped between 20 and 95. Items below it are never drafted.

effective = clamp(round(platform threshold + model offset), 20, 95)

  • Not every item that clears the line is drafted: only the top scorers, one per run by default, after a check that no article from the last 7 days has a near-identical title.
  • When nothing clears it, the article queue suggests a lower setting: five under the current line or five under the top quartile's score, whichever is lower, and never below 30.
  • Right before each draft, the workspace's count of AI generations this month is checked against its plan.

This gate covers the article queue's runs. The scheduled daily run, described in stage 1, doesn't pass through it.

ClearedAbove this line a run has spent a one-token probe and one model call per scored item. Below it, each draft takes at least three more calls, and ends as a draft for an editor, not a published article.

5Draft in the workspace's voice, in English and Arabic

The workspace's AI profile goes in front of the drafting prompt as one line of guidelines: voice, reading level, target length, keywords and the citation rule. Its custom rules go at the end.

  • English body first, then an Arabic version with up to two attempts and a title-only translation if both fail, then three alternative headlines for an editor to choose from.
  • That is at least three model calls per draft and up to six, not counting retries, against one to score, which is why the line sits before drafting.
  • Each draft records the model, provider, tokens and cost. If every model call fell back, the model field is left empty rather than naming a model that wrote nothing.
  • The result is saved with the status drafted.

6Leave publishing to an editor

Nothing the pipeline writes is published by the pipeline. Drafts wait in the article queue for someone with the Editor role or above.

  • Approve, one at a time or in bulk, sets the status to published. Reject sets it to rejected and hides the article.
  • Public article lists, the sitemap and the public API leave drafts out.
  • Workspaces have Owner, Admin, Editor and Viewer roles, and each has its own AI profile, plan and Stripe subscription.

What runs where

Every piece below is in the deployed backend definition; nothing here is planned work. One Amplify app builds the backend and the server-rendered front end together, in one job per deploy of the main branch.

Amplify Hosting

The Angular and Ionic app, rendered on the server by a Node 20 compute function, with static files served alongside.

Cognito

Sign-in by email or phone, with optional SMS or authenticator-app MFA. After sign-up, onboarding creates the user's workspace and makes them its Owner.

AppSync GraphQL

25 data models, plus custom mutations backed by Lambda: start a run, manage members, check out, open the billing portal.

Lambda

20 functions. The pipeline runs in one of them, with a 900-second limit and 1 GB of memory. The others include membership, billing, email, webhooks, account deletion, the public API, the sitemap and the scheduled jobs.

DynamoDB

The tables behind the GraphQL models. The pipeline writes its job, queue and article rows directly.

EventBridge

Three rules: scheduled generation at 08:00 UTC, trial reminders at 09:00 UTC, and a publishing check every 5 minutes.

Stripe

Checkout for subscriptions and the customer portal. A webhook with a verified signature keeps each workspace's plan and status in step.

S3 and SES

Article images in S3, and transactional email through SES.

Not in this build

Scoring on scheduled runs
The daily run writes from a topic and never meets the threshold. Only runs from the article queue are scored.
A threshold per workspace
The base threshold is one platform setting. A workspace moves its line only by choosing a model.
Live progress
The page polls the job record every 1.5 seconds. Nothing is pushed to it.
A spending cap in dollars
Plans cap the number of AI generations a month. They don't cap what those generations cost.
