The stages and the threshold formula are the real ones.

  1. Sources

    You point it at RSS feeds and topics, per workspace.

  2. Scrape

    A Lambda fetches each source and queues candidate items.

  3. Score

    The model you pick rates each item against your editorial rubric and a quality threshold, adjusted per model so cheaper models aren't silently rejected.

  4. Draft

    Items that pass are drafted in your editorial voice: voice, reading level, word count, keywords and citation rules.

  5. Publish

    You review the queue, edit if you want, and publish.

Houseplants need less water and more light as winter sets in. Moving pots closer to a bright window, while keeping leaves clear of cold glass, helps most common varieties through the darker months. Watering can wait until the top of the soil feels dry.

Your plants are probably a bit sleepy right now, and that's fine. Give them the brightest window you've got, keep them off the cold glass, and go easy on the watering. They'll perk up again once spring rolls around.

During the winter months, houseplants require less frequent watering and greater access to natural light. Pots should be positioned near a bright window, away from direct contact with cold glass, and the soil should be allowed to dry between waterings.

Shorter days in winter slow the growth of most houseplants, which in turn lowers their water requirements. Care routines should therefore prioritize light over watering frequency: plants placed near bright windows, but away from cold glass, are less likely to suffer leaf damage during this period.

So, your plants look a little tired lately? That's normal for this time of year. With shorter days they grow slowly, so they need less water than you'd think. Move them somewhere bright, keep them away from the cold glass, and check the soil before you water.
